# Rater packet HL1 — human rater, checkability judgments (n = 23)

You are a rater in a study of software-specification rules. This packet
is everything you may use. Ground rules:

- **One sitting, roughly 30 minutes.** Pen and paper or a text file.
- **Use only this packet.** No web search, no AI assistance, no
  discussing the items with anyone until you have returned your
  answers.
- Below are 23 numbered rules taken from a published Internet standard
  for calendar files (the ".ics" files calendar apps exchange). Each
  rule constrains a calendar file or the programs that produce and
  consume calendar files. The item numbers are their positions in a
  larger corpus; gaps are expected and mean nothing.
- Some rules use format jargon (property names like "DTSTART",
  "RRULE", "BYDAY"). You do not need to know what these mean precisely
  — treat them as named fields inside the file, and judge the SHAPE of
  the rule.

**The question — answer it for every item:**

> Could a computer program decide whether this rule was followed by
> examining ONLY the complete calendar file it applies to — with no
> other information: no knowledge of what the file's author meant or
> intended, no other files or messages, no clock or calendar of
> real-world events, and no visibility into what any program or person
> actually did?
>
> **YES** = a checker reading the file alone could always deliver the
> right verdict.
> **NO** = delivering the right verdict can require information beyond
> the file.

Two worked examples from a different domain (books, not calendars):

- "Every chapter number in the book MUST be even." — **YES**: a
  program can read the book and check every chapter number.
- "The author MUST have written the book without consulting a
  dictionary." — **NO**: nothing in the book's text can prove or
  disprove what the author consulted.

- Answer EVERY item. Return one line per item, in the form
  `NUMBER:YES` or `NUMBER:NO` (e.g. `8:NO`). If you are genuinely
  torn, give your best single answer with a trailing `?`
  (e.g. `104:YES?`). (Numbers in these two examples are arbitrary and
  do not appear below.)
