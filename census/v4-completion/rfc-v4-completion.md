## Findings

1. **The key-lifecycle repair worked — the band-breaking corpus now
   agrees best of all.** V5's 5/4 split, which sided with NEITHER v3
   rater, reproduced exactly in both families, including all five items
   the pack does not name. RFC 9001 — the corpus whose v3 agreement
   broke the series' floor at 76.8%, with ten of sixteen disagreements
   on exactly this boundary — moved to 94.2% cross-family raw agreement
   under v4, and its between-rater share gap narrowed from 13.1 points
   (53.6/66.7) to 2.9 (60.9/58.0). Rule 18's gated-object discriminator
   transmits.

2. **V2 failed on the only item that could have carried it.** The
   protocol disclosed in advance that rule 16's examples settle eight of
   the nine predicted items; all eight landed DOMAIN, as told. The one
   un-named item — 191, "MUST ignore an ICMP message that claims the
   PMTU has decreased below…" — landed PROCESS in BOTH raters: they read
   the ignore-duty as conduct, not as the datum bound the author saw in
   it. Graded FAIL per the pre-commitment. The seam it exposes
   (ignore/reject duties whose trigger is a datum bound — is the
   classified predicate the bound or the conduct?) is recorded as an
   observation for a future version, not adjudicated.

3. **V7(b) failed 6/6 while V7(a) passed 6/6 — the count-bound clause is
   mis-designed, and the grade stands.** Two failure modes, both now
   measured: (a) zero-spread classes carry zero tolerance — MLS fails
   its loose bounds on THRESHOLD because ONE outside item (42, a
   both-v3-raters TYPESTATE) moved, against an archived pair that agreed
   exactly ([1,1]); 9001 likewise fails on TYPESTATE (21/22 against a
   [20, 20] pair). Not every excursion is that formalism: MLS Xv4's
   TYPESTATE (33, against loose bound 32) and 9001 Xv4's DOMAIN (13,
   against loose bound 15) are real nonzero-spread excursions;
   (b) on QUIC the movement is broad and includes
   direction-opposing drift: Av4's outside-set TYPESTATE fell to 113
   against [128,133] and its THRESHOLD rose to 24 against [17,20], while
   both raters together moved six THRESHOLD items to DOMAIN
   ({68, 86, 93, 186, 187, 188}) — **rule 16 generalizing past its named
   items, the same phenomenon as rule 17's sub-bucket reach in
   `census/v4-tls/`** — and eight TYPESTATE items to PROCESS
   ({11, 19, 29, 54, 55, 105, 261, 272}). A v5 owes a redesigned
   no-regression clause (per-item scoped, tolerance floors for
   zero-spread classes); until then V7(b)'s six FAILs are the record.

4. **QUIC is the honest negative: v4 did not improve its transmission.**
   Av4-vs-Xv4 raw agreement (84.0%) sits BELOW the v3 pair (85.1%), the
   only corpus where v4 agreement is not the best on record; Av4's share
   (63.7%) fell below the v3 band while Xv4's (68.7%) stayed inside it.
   The churn concentrates on the PROCESS boundary (full-corpus PROCESS:
   Av4 54 vs the v3 pair's 48/32) — rule 18's lifecycle vocabulary
   appears to pull procedure readings beyond key/phase contexts. This
   also disposes of the amendment's concentration clause for QUIC:
   agreement gains could not concentrate in the named items because
   there were no net gains. Recorded as v4's measured cost, alongside
   its measured gains (findings 1 and 5).

5. **Across the completion, cross-family consensus against BOTH v3
   raters is rare outside QUIC — on the V7 outside sets**: 2 items on
   MLS (17: U→PROCESS; 42: TYPESTATE→THRESHOLD), 1 on RFC 9001 (33:
   DOMAIN→TYPESTATE), 16 on QUIC (the two clusters in finding 3 plus
   81: CV→PROCESS and 235: DOMAIN→TYPESTATE). Over the full corpora the
   counts are 5 / 2 / 16 — the extra items ({90, 113, 114} on MLS, {34}
   on 9001) are prediction-named flips TO the predicted labels,
   excluded above because the pack steered them. MLS and 9001 under v4 sit close to their v3
   readings with sharply higher agreement (88.2% vs 85.0%; 94.2% vs
   76.8%); the spectrum's shape is unchanged in the v4 series
   (≈59% → ≈64–69% → ≈58–61% for the crypto sibling — version-labeled,
   never mixed with v3 numbers).
